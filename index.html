About RMC

The Rwanda Muslim Community (RMC), formerly known as the Association des Musulmans au Rwanda (AMUR), is the apex body representing the Islamic faith and interests in Rwanda. Historically a marginalized group, the community has seen a significant shift in its social and political standing in the decades following the 1994 Genocide against the Tutsi.

The RMC is the primary organization representing the Islamic faith in Rwanda. Headquartered in Kigali, it operates under the leadership of the Mufti of Rwanda.

Historical Context and Evolution

The RMC has been central to the religious and social life of Muslims in Rwanda, providing a structured framework for worship and community development. A significant part of its modern history involves the effort to make sacred texts accessible to the Rwandan people in their native language, Kinyarwanda. This journey reached a major milestone with the publication of the first translation of the Quran in 2011, refined in 2020 for higher accuracy.

Mission and Vision

  • Spiritual Guidance: Providing a "path and guidance" for the inhabitants of the world through the teachings of the Quran.
  • Educational Advancement: Collaborating with international partners like the Africa Development and Education Foundation (ADEF) to improve religious literacy.
  • Community Support: Offering religious resources, such as the translated Quran, free of charge to the public.

The Translation of the Holy Quran

In partnership with ADEF, the RMC successfully completed two major versions:

  1. The Older Version (2011): The first major attempt to bring meanings to Kinyarwanda speakers. Groundbreaking but required refinement.
  2. The New Version (November 2020): A specialized committee worked from Feb 2014 to Nov 2020. Improved contextual accuracy, enhanced clarity with footnotes, and linguistic precision. Validated by over sixty scholars.

Acknowledgements

Led by Sheikh Hitimana Salim (former Mufti) and the Translation Committee led by Sheikh Mushumba Yunusu, with support from ADEF and the Islamic Cultural Center in Nyamirambo.

Post-Genocide Growth & Future Vision

Since 1994, the Muslim population grew significantly as many Rwandans converted, citing the community's ethical stance during the genocide and commitment to peace. The current Strategic Plan (2024–2029) focuses on Unity, Dawah, Socio-economic development, infrastructure renovation, and governance reforms.

Our Key Programs

Driving socio-economic development and spiritual growth.

Education and Schools

The RMC operates numerous private Islamic schools across Rwanda, such as Lycee Islamique de Rwamagana. These integrate the national curriculum (REB) with Islamic religious studies, managed via the digital "RMC Education" system.

Peace and Reconciliation

A key partner in Rwanda’s "Ndi Umunyarwanda" initiative. Muslim leaders actively serve on national interfaith commissions, and host events like the International Qur'an Recitation Competition to promote cohesion.

Social & Cultural Life

Organizing major Eid celebrations recognized nationally. Empowering Muslim youth to participate actively in monthly Umuganda (community work) focused on environmental protection and development.
